# Enterprise Generative AI Use Policy

## 1. Approved AI Services
Employees may use only generative AI services approved by the company for company work. Consumer AI accounts must not be used for confidential company information.

## 2. Sensitive and Personal Data
Employees must not paste passwords, API keys, access tokens, customer personal data, employee personal data, payment card data, or other confidential information into an AI service unless that specific service and use case have been formally approved for such data.

## 3. Human Review
AI-generated content used for customer communication, legal interpretation, financial decisions, hiring decisions, security decisions, or other high-impact business activities must be reviewed by an appropriately qualified employee before use.

## 4. Grounding and Source Verification
When an AI system answers questions from enterprise documents, the application should provide source references where practical. Users must verify important policy, legal, financial, and security claims against the authoritative source before taking high-impact action.

## 5. Prompt Injection and Untrusted Content
Content retrieved from websites, emails, uploaded files, and enterprise documents must be treated as untrusted input. AI applications must not treat instructions embedded in retrieved content as higher-priority application instructions. Systems handling sensitive workflows should include controls for prompt injection and data exfiltration attempts.

## 6. Logging and Monitoring
Production AI applications should log security-relevant events, model failures, blocked requests, and evaluation results in accordance with company logging and retention requirements. Logs must not unnecessarily store secrets or sensitive personal data.
